# New Forge game review — 2026-10-29-2108

Six deliberately selected informative completed games: four from the owned-engine candidate and two from the newest physical-baseline calibration control. This is a case review, not a random sample or a new win-rate estimate. No games or watcher/engine processes were changed.

## Full-run context

The owned-engine candidate swaps Synthetic Destiny → Dreadhorde Invasion and Overwrite the Multiverse → Darksteel Angel, preserving lands/rocks. It finished **40 wins / 100 started games, with 3 suspect draws** (40/97 excluding those draws), versus the physical baseline’s 30/100. Independently matching non-suspect pod/game keys gives **94 pairs: owned-engine 38 wins, baseline 29**; discordant counts are 24 owned-only versus 15 baseline-only. Claude reports paired p≈0.20. This is a promising candidate, not an established improvement. The package also replaces cards the legacy AI underuses; it does not isolate either new card’s effect or prove those human-play cards should be cut.

The exile candidate finished 36/100; it is not the focus of these six cases. New C7 natural-draw calibration is separate from these candidate runs. A runtime named “combo” is not itself proof that real-rules/combo-pilot flags are enabled.

## Combo implementation progress

Claude’s fixture report says 10/10 deterministic diagnostics passed. I inspected the legal-loop fixture itself: its telemetry records seven alternating loop steps, a stop/finish, an Ajani haste resolution and actual Cadet attacks. Its result is explicitly diagnostic and not scored. That establishes an engineered scenario, not how often the physical deck naturally finds the pieces.

The report openly lists missing interruption/proliferation fixtures. Natural-draw real-rules and strategy-aware comparisons still need their own results. Do not turn a fixture win or this Arm A calibration into a new power rating.

## Practical takeaways

- Dreadhorde has a concrete observed route from early enchantment to replacement Army to Jace fodder. This strengthens its case as a trial for Patrick’s real token-shortage concern.
- Darksteel’s survival effect is visible, but the very repetitive long log merits a runtime-progress audit before treating that example as uncomplicated performance evidence.
- Memnarch can make a winning-looking attack lose before damage. Look at the loss event and library count, not just which opposing commander eventually won.
- Existing Ajani/Saheeli/Way/walker token infrastructure can still win games. Do not assume every candidate win comes from its new cards.
- No physical swap is implied by these case reviews. The strategy’s keep constraints and the difference between AI-unsupported cards and weak human-play cards still apply.
