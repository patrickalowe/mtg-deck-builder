### Why Aetherize was kept

The user specifically asked **why Aetherize had been “unrecommended.”** Explanation given: it was only **deprioritized**, not rejected. It is exceptionally good as a 4-mana instant against attackers, including Elf tokens (which disappear when returned to hand). It can defend the user and create breathing room for Jace/planeswalkers. Downsides: attackers with valuable ETB effects can be replayed; does nothing against noncombat damage, ETB triggers or direct damage; doesn't permanently eliminate nontoken attackers. Do not treat Aetherize and Cyclonic Rift as substitutes by default: Rift is broad offensive/defensive nonland interaction; Aetherize is dedicated combat insurance.

Earlier comparison versus **Settle the Wreckage** was an exploratory model: Aetherize **75/500 four-player model wins (15.0%)**, Settle **73/500 (14.6%)** in one simplified scenario. **The two-game difference is noise.** Settle exiles attacking creatures but may give opponents basic lands; Aetherize bounces and preserves their creatures in hand. There is no conclusive measured winner.

### Purchase prioritization / value

Historically favored priority cards for the protective engine included `Ripples of Potential`, `Clever Concealment`, `Oath of Gideon`, `Lim-Dûl's Vault`, and `Divergent Transformations` before splashier upgrades. Latest list now also includes stronger removal/tempo in `Cyclonic Rift`, `Aetherize`, `Massacre Wurm`, and `Chandra's Ignition`. Prices change; look up current prices and actual printings rather than reusing old price estimates.

### Jace matchup lessons

- **Lathril / creature swarms:** early blockers, `Aetherize`, `Massacre Wurm`, `Cyclonic Rift`, and properly timed sweepers are valuable.
- **Giada / Angels:** defend Jace from flying attackers; counter/targeted removal and flexible mass answers important. Angels often resist low-damage wipes.
- **Miirym / Dragons:** protect against/stop Miirym, avoid allowing repeat Dragon ETB/copy engines; `Aetherize` alone cannot stop noncombat triggers.
- **General:** invest in dependable colored mana, fodder, planeswalker protection, draw/tutoring, and conversion to a victory. Be careful not to dilute payoff density by adding too many small creatures.

### 10.3 Core tactical lessons from all Wakanda comparisons

- Build **early artifact density and mana curve** rather than leaning entirely on expensive payoff cards; `Foundry Inspector`, `Mystic Forge`, `Forsaken Monument` were considered for this reason.
- Board safety against sweeper/removal and attack deterrence matters in multiplayer: `Clever Concealment`, `Reconnaissance`, `Ghostly Prison`, and `Akroma's Will` occupy different defensive/offensive niches.
- Choose **efficient exile answers** (`Path to Exile`, `Winds of Abandon`) while noting opponent land-ramp tradeoffs; don't overvalue slow high-MV removal (`Meteor Golem`) if turn tempo matters.
- `Nettlecyst`, equipment, and artifact count can scale commander damage for faster kills; the decision depends on how often a wide board survives, equipment stays attached, and the unique advantage of The Spear's other abilities.
- Against **Lathril:** mass swarms and speed are a weakness. Against **Giada:** protect yourself from fast fliers and growing bodies. Against **Miirym:** prevent duplicate Dragon ETBs; pressure opponents before they dominate the late game.
- The deck is intended to **balance 1v1 and four-player play**, not become a mono-focus optimized only for the most favorable modeled matchup.
